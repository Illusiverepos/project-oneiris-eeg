Two-Stage Cortical Recovery in Clinical PTSD, Independent Replication in Human Fear Extinction, and an Anti-Startle Architecture for Non-Waking Nightmare Therapy

Austin Rutan • Independent Researcher (Project ONEIRIS) • rutanaus000@gmail.com
Code & Reproducible Pipeline: https://github.com/Illusiverepos/project-oneiris-eeg • Preprint Paper 3 (Labs #1–#12) • November 2026
Abstract. Post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD) traps military veterans and trauma survivors in decades-long cycles of insomnia and recurrent nightmares that resist spontaneous extinction. Extending the 3-axis cortical coordinate system established across 10 independent anesthesia, sleep, lucid-dreaming, and psychedelic labs (Papers 1–2: posterior 1/f excitability S_phys, external reality anchor R_tether, and the Right_Frontal executive switch), we test an Inverted-Switch Model of PTSD Sleep across two new human EEG laboratories (Labs #11–#12), a 38-year longitudinal combat-veteran dream archive, and a calibrated biophysical model. First, auditing the 38-year DreamBank Vietnam Vet combat series (1970–2008, n = 98 vs. 22,317 controls) confirms chronic combat threat replay (71.4% vs. 13.4%, OR = 15.97, p = 1.86×10−61), a wake-snap trap (24.3% of threat dreams violently awaken the sleeper, representing 94.4% of all awakenings; OR = 2.21, p = 0.0089), zero spontaneous lucidity (0/98), and zero spontaneous extinction across four decades (75.5% → 67.4%, p = 0.502). Second, in Lab #11 (Mendeley 10.17632/gsxphk87mc, n = 20 clinical PTSD survivors with 100% same-device wearable EEG across ~120 recordings; n = 29 DSM-5 PCL-5 sleep outcomes), neurofeedback remission (PCL-5 38.9 → 11.0; nightmares −75%, dz = 1.743, p < 0.0001; insomnia −67%, dz = 2.121, p < 0.0001; Motor-Imagery and Waitlist controls null) follows a Two-Stage Cortical Recovery: Stage 1 (Early → Mid) selectively steepens Right − Left Frontal 1/f slope (84% of total shift; 9/10 remitted patients overall, dz = −0.667, p = 0.0349, sign p = 0.0215; MI control 4/10, p = 0.430) while re-synchronizing fragmented dimensionality (globalEffDimNorm ↓, dz = −0.941, p = 0.0029); Stage 2 (Mid → Late) releases posterior temporal persistence (postDFA ↓, dz = −0.971, p = 0.0021). Moreover, 9/10 remitted patients acutely steepened Right_Frontal 1/f within a single 30-minute session (dz = −0.777, p = 0.0140). Third, in Lab #12 (OpenNeuro ds007114, n = 28 healthy humans), active fear extinction independently replicates the exact quantitative Posterior DFA α release (−0.070, dz = −0.465, p = 0.0138, 19/28, matching Lab #11 −0.074) while trait anxiety blunts frontal extinction updating (ρ = −0.375, p = 0.0391). Finally, a Locus Coeruleus Wilson–Cowan model reproduces PTSD NREM right-frontal 1/f flattening and defines an Anti-Startle Goldilocks Corridor for non-waking Imagery Rehearsal Therapy + Targeted Memory Reactivation (IRT+TMR), implemented in an open-source closed-loop engine (--mode irt-tmr).

1. Introduction: The Inverted-Switch Model of PTSD Sleep

Across 10 independent human EEG laboratories (Papers 1–2), we mapped a 3-axis physical coordinate system of human consciousness: (1) posterior cortical excitability (S_phys), (2) external sensory anchoring (R_tether = +z(effDim_post) + z(DFA α) − z(logBeta), separating internal vs. external reality in 45/56 humans, p < 0.0001), and (3) a Right_Frontal (F4/F8) executive switch that stays offline in normal NREM dreaming (0/3) and switches ON selectively during lucid REM control (11/12, p = 0.0190).

Healthy REM sleep supports overnight fear extinction because Locus Coeruleus (LC) norepinephrine drops near zero. In combat PTSD, persistent nocturnal LC norepinephrine creates an Inverted Switch: high-density sleep EEG (de Boer et al., 2020) shows elevated fast activity (flatter 1/f) most pronounced over Right_Frontal sensors during NREM (driving insomnia) paired with an opposing posterior shift during REM (driving nightmares). Convergently, 1 Hz inhibitory rTMS over right DLPFC (F4) reduces combat PTSD symptoms in veterans (Kozel et al., 2018, N = 103). Here we test whether clinical PTSD recovery and active human fear extinction reverse these exact axes, and translate the constraints into an anti-startle closed-loop IRT+TMR architecture.

3. Biophysical Model & Anti-Startle Non-Waking IRT+TMR Engineering

Adding Locus Coeruleus norepinephrine (lcNE) to our two-layer Wilson–Cowan model (N = 20 trials/regime) reproduced PTSD NREM right-frontal 1/f flattening (−1.503 → −0.854), slow-wave collapse (0.515 → 0.111), and right-frontal beta tripling (0.091 → 0.273). In PTSD REM (meanThreat = 0.974, wakeSnapPct = 100%, extinctionIdx = 0.000), gentle IRT+TMR (tmrCueAmp = 0.25) reduced threat to 0.635 with 0% wake-snaps and restored extinction (0.232), whereas loud cues (≥ 0.60) triggered acoustic startle (wakeSnapPct = 6.7%–16.7%) and collapsed extinction (0.242 → 0.156).

Implementing this Anti-Startle Corridor in toolkit/cueing-system/closed_loop_engine.js (--mode irt-tmr) and replaying real human REM (TWC_USA case15_sub108.edf, 1,552 s) delivered 1 whisper-gentle chord during calm REM (rfBetaFrac = 0.18, alphaFrac = 0.17), held PTSD_NREM_WATCHTOWER_HOLD, and executed 13 automatic anti-startle WAKE_GUARD vetoes on beta spikes. An accompanying standalone 3-phase full-night prototype (irt_tmr_prototype.html) pairs 6-bpm vagal pacing, safe-ending IRT with a 0.65 s fade-in C-major7 pad, and optional early-NREM pink-noise shielding.

Limitations & Clinical Scope: Cross-subject single-site Spearman correlations with ΔPCL-5 in Lab #11 were non-significant after strict same-device pairing (p = 0.07–0.99), demonstrating that within-subject Right − Left Frontal asymmetry is the robust biomarker. Lab #11 comprises female trauma survivors and warrants prospective validation in combat veterans. All ONEIRIS software tools are open-source research prototypes, not medical devices, and do not substitute for VA or clinician-guided PTSD treatment.

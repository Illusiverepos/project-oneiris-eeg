Two-Electrode Forehead EEG Screening of Biological Brain Aging, Alzheimer's Disease, and Frontotemporal Dementia via Wiener–Khinchin Invariants

Author: Austin Rutan (Independent Researcher, Project ONEIRIS) · rutanaus000@gmail.com
Preprint Series: Project ONEIRIS — Paper 6 (November 2026) · Code: https://github.com/Illusiverepos/project-oneiris-eeg (Non-Commercial Academic & Research License)
Abstract. Longitudinal monitoring of biological brain aging and early neurodegenerative slowing remains bottlenecked by expensive neuroimaging and 64-channel clinical EEG rigs because single dry forehead electrodes suffer from skull and impedance noise. Here we validate a zero-dependency two-electrode forehead EEG biomarker suite (oneiris_wearable_sdk.js) anchored on the Wiener–Khinchin Violation Index (Δ_WK = slopeBB + 2·DFA_α − 1) across two independent international cohorts (N = 187 unique adults, Labs #13–#14). In Cohort 1 (OpenNeuro ds005555 BOAS, 127 overnight dry-forehead AF7/AF8 nights deduplicated to N = 99 unique adults, ages 18–82), raw REM slopeBB carried zero aging signal (p = 0.966), whereas REM Δ_WK tracked chronological age controlling for Sex and BMI at r_partial = −0.2969, p = 0.0022 (Wake DFA α p = 0.00024; N3 slopeBB ρ = +0.395, p = 0.00036), and a 100% PSG-free dual-channel Brain-Age Clock achieved strictly out-of-sample 5-fold Ridge CV r = 0.4032, p = 1.4 × 10−5 (MAE = 12.21 yrs). In Cohort 2 (OpenNeuro ds004504, N = 88 clinical humans: 36 AD, 23 FTD, 29 Controls), just 60 seconds of eyes-closed Fp1/Fp2 forehead EEG separated AD (AUC = 0.804, d = 1.205, p < 10−6) and FTD (AUC = 0.826, d = 1.298, p = 1.1 × 10−5) via Theta/Alpha Ratio, tracked clinical MMSE (r = −0.392, p = 7.6 × 10−5), and revealed a clinical double dissociation: FTD selectively broke frontal Δ_WK (d = +0.753, p = 0.0086) while sparing betaFrac (p = 0.471), whereas AD selectively lost betaFrac (d = −0.838, p = 0.00093) while sparing Δ_WK (p = 0.526). Strictly out-of-sample 5-fold CV achieved AUC = 0.7849 (76.3% sensitivity, 79.3% specificity, permutation p = 0.0005) for Dementia vs. Controls and out-of-sample MMSE prediction (p = 0.0056), while honestly showing 60-second 2-electrode FTD vs. AD subtype classification (AUC = 0.595, p = 0.122) is not yet sufficient for clinical differential diagnosis alone.

5-fold out-of-sample MMSE cognitive score prediction across all 88 humans reached r = 0.2860, p = 0.0056 (MAE = 4.62 points) on 2 forehead electrodes. Adding the 2 behind-the-ear temporoparietal electrodes of a standard 4-channel consumer headband (Fp1/Fp2 + T5/T6 = AF7/AF8 + TP9/TP10) boosted Alzheimer's vs. Controls by +0.111 AUC to 0.8784 (80.6% sensitivity, 89.7% specificity, permutation p = 0.0005), raised Dementia vs. Controls to AUC = 0.8270 (86.2% specificity), and strengthened out-of-sample MMSE prediction to r = 0.3778, p = 0.00015 (MAE = 4.28), while FTD vs. Controls stayed saturated on frontal electrodes (AUC = 0.7871) and 60-second FTD vs. AD remained null (AUC = 0.565).
